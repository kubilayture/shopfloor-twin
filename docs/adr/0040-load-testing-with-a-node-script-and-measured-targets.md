# ADR-0040: Load testing with a Node script, targets judged per run

- Status: accepted (#245, P9-18)
- Date: 2026-10-20
- Deciders: Claude, within the Phase 9 plan ("Performance"; review decisions round 1 findings 2, 4, 12, 13 and 22, round 2 finding 5, round 3 finding 4 and the final check); recorded for the owner under the standing instruction of 2026-10-15

## Context and problem statement

The master plan (section 9) asks for "ingest throughput at 60× simulation speed with all machines; UI update latency", with the tool "decided in Phase 9"; section 12 left the load-testing tool and the performance targets open. Until Phase 9 performance was measured piecemeal (ingest about 1,000 stored messages/s in the runbook, the push budgets of Phases 4–8, `pnpm test:trace-perf`), with no ingest lag metric and no stated targets. The Phase 9 plan set targets T1–T6, each with the run that measures it, on the hardened stack (SignAndEncrypt, HTTPS, dynamic security) in a Colima VM of 4 CPUs and 4 GiB. This ADR records the tool, how each target is measured and judged, the new MES metrics and what the runs showed.

## Considered options

1. **k6** (Grafana's load tool): a new binary or image, JavaScript but not Node, MQTT only through an extension, WebSockets with its own API.
2. **A Node script** in `e2e/` (`pnpm test:perf`, as `pnpm test:trace-perf`): Node's built-in `fetch` and `WebSocket`, the repository's clients and judges, no new dependency.

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: 2. The plant itself is the main load (the simulator at 60× through OPC UA, Modbus, the gateway and the broker); what a tool adds is 20 HTTP and WebSocket users, which `fetch` and `WebSocket` do, and the judging, which needs the repository's latency judge (`e2e/src/latency.ts`), Compose stacks (`e2e/src/stack.ts`), the simulator's control API and its plant events. k6 would duplicate those or call out to them.

### The suite

`pnpm test:perf` (`e2e/src/perf.ts`, under the heavy lock) runs three runs, each on its own disposable Compose project with ephemeral ports and the observability profile's Prometheus only (`--profile observability`; Grafana's page cache would count against T5's 80 % rule, #263's review), torn down with `down --volumes` with `compose.observability.yml` loaded; the images it built and Prometheus's image, if it pulled it, are removed at the end.

- **P1, steady:** `orders-01`'s Monday shift A in orders mode at 60×, released and dispatched through the API while the simulator waits at 06:00 (each command confirmed by its device fact), the scenario's injections posted, then resumed. The window runs from the MES's catch-up (the first moment after the resume with `mes_ingest_pending_messages` ≤ 100 and the gateway's outbox empty) to 14:00. P1 also measures the live message rate (every outcome of `mes_ingest_messages_total` over the window) that P3 must offer three times over.
- **P2, drain:** the same start; the MES stopped with SIGTERM at 07:00 for **15** wall minutes (fifteen simulated hours; coordinator decision 2) and started.
- **P3, headroom:** no simulator and no gateway; `week-01`'s golden feed of Monday and two more hours (26 simulated hours, so every machine has a fact after shift C) published by the gated `mes-server` Vitest entry `apps/mes-server/src/perf-replay.e2e.test.ts` (`PERF_REPLAY_BROKER_URL`, the broker's port published for this run only by `e2e/compose.perf-replay.yml`) at 180×, as the gateway's MQTT user, each message's `receivedAt` set to its publication; beside the facts, every `UNS_TELEMETRY` metric at three times the gateway's live cadence (coordinator decision 1). The feed is the golden harness's, so `e2e/src/perf.ts` imports no MES code (review round 1, finding 22).

### New MES metrics

- `mes_ingest_lag_seconds{class}` (`state`, `cycle`, `alarm`, `job`, `telemetry`): the gateway's `receivedAt` to the MES's commit, observed right after the commit, only for live device facts stored for the first time over the session (never `deliver()`'s replays); edges as above, so T1 and T6 are judged exactly on 0.25, 1 and 5 s.
- `mes_ingest_lag_excluded_total{reason}`: `retained` (a retained copy of a fact topic), `anchor` (a retained state or alarm delivery), `snapshot` (a state or alarm snapshot), `duplicate` (a dedup hit). There is no `replayed` reason: ingest can't tell a delivery from the gateway's store-and-forward buffer from a live one, so P1 asserts no replay can be in the window (review round 3, finding 4).
- `mes_ingest_lag_max_seconds`: the largest observation of the last 5 to 10 s, since a histogram has no max (#263's review).
- `mes_ingest_oldest_pending_age_seconds`: now minus the `receivedAt` of the delivery MQTT.js has handed to the MES and the MES has not committed; 0 when it holds none. MQTT.js hands over one delivery at a time, so behind a backlog (on the socket or in the broker) this is the backlog head's age: unlike the count below, it grows with any backlog. The resilience suite (#243, #268) still uses its stand-in (the MES's plant clock at most 60 simulated seconds behind) for the pending check; it can move to this gauge later.
- `mes_ingest_pending_messages` (diagnostic): PUBLISH packets read from the session's socket and not yet committed, counted by a reader of MQTT's fixed headers (`modules/ingest/wire.ts`). **Bounded by the receive maximum (100)** the broker honours, and the socket is read only while MQTT.js keeps up, so it can't show a backlog beyond that (Mosquitto reports no per-client queue); P2's drain peaked at 47.

### Coordinator decisions (review of PR #269)

The owner asked the coordinator not to stop for questions; the coordinator took these calls on the review of PR #269, and they are listed for the owner's review (STATUS):

1. **T6's load.** The golden feed has no telemetry, so 180× alone offered about P1's live rate, not three times it (P1 measures 13.35 msg/s; the first runs' "17/s" counted P2's published messages over its whole span, an error). P3 now adds every `UNS_TELEMETRY` metric at three times the gateway's live cadence (OPC UA variables sampled every 1 s and the washer polled every 250 ms, so every 333 ms and 83 ms), keeping the golden facts' pacing; its offered rate must be at least three times P1's measured live rate, and its publisher may run at most 1 s late.
2. **T2's rate and window.** The first runs divided the count at the catch-up by a window measured across a slower loop, which biased the rate upward, over a 4 s drain. Now: a 100 ms `/metrics`-only sampler, the lower of the median of consecutive slopes and the mean over the steady drain (the coordinator's re-review: neither alone may carry it), the catch-up and finality in their own loop, and the MES stopped for 15 minutes instead of the plan's 5 (plan "Review decisions", P9-18 implementation). At the measured live rate that is about 12,000 messages, an 11 s drain, not the 15 s the review estimated; the median rests on 92 slopes.
3. **The pending check.** `mes_ingest_pending_messages` ≤ 100 can't fail (above), so T1's hour-boundary check is `mes_ingest_oldest_pending_age_seconds` ≤ 1 s; the count stays a diagnostic.
4. **T1's "gateway buffer 0 over the window".** Every UNS message passes through the gateway's SQLite outbox (ADR-0014: committed, sent, deleted after its PUBACK, up to 100 in flight and 20 ms commit batches), so a 1 s sample of live traffic sees a few messages without any replay (the Q3 baseline: 7 at most). A replay needs the broker session lost or the gateway restarted, so the window is judged on the broker session up at every sample, the depth within one live window (≤ 200), nothing dropped, and T5's restarts; the depth is reported. The end-of-run check reads the outbox until it empties within 10 s of the pause.
5. **T5 can't pass on a dead container:** every container from `ps --all` (above). T1 also requires no duplicate in the window; T4 every read 200 and every kind's p95.

### Consequences

- Good: every target has a run, a method and a pass rule written down, judged by the script, with the VM, host load and image digests in `e2e/results/perf/results.json`; the exit code fails on any miss, and a target is never relaxed silently (Q3 first; the method changes are decisions on the record).
- Good: the lag histogram, its exclusions and the oldest pending age make the ingest path's delay visible in Prometheus for every later change; the runbook has the PromQL for each target.
- Bad: T3 and T4 are client side and their truth's clock is the event's arrival at the harness, not the simulator's emission: a few milliseconds of WebSocket transit count against the MES.
- Bad: P3's extra load is telemetry, the cheapest message class to store; the facts keep the golden pacing. Three times the facts would change the golden run's timing; the plan's T6 keeps the feed.
- Bad: `mes_ingest_pending_messages` is bounded by the receive maximum (above).
- Neutral: the scalability follow-ups of #245's comments (`OrderOee.lineOrders` bounded to the open window, `GET /oee/line/orders` paginated, the `#finals` maps evicted) are not required by T4 or T5 at shift scale (every kind of read p95 ≤ 55.5 ms, the MES at 0.149 core and 27.6 % of its memory limit); they stay open for month-scale data.
