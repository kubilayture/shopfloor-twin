# Phase 06 – Alarms and shifts: log

Dated entries, one per merged pull request: what merged, what was learned, deviations from the plan.

## 2026-10-16

- Phase 6 planned: issues #138–#148, milestone "Phase 06 – Alarms and shifts".
- Merged #149 (#138): Phase 6 plan, revised twice after review (failure injection only in `Running` and held otherwise, revisions over every hour a late fact can change, old-epoch anchors and snapshots, lost clears closed by resync snapshots).
- #139: `@shopfloor/alarm-core` and the alarm definitions (ADR-0024; ADR-0005 amended).
  - The KPIs per bucket and scope as the plan defines them, in integers and `oee-core`'s BigInt fractions; `exactnessRules` now covers `alarm-core`. A bucket is any range on whole UTC hours, so a shift or a `no-shift` stretch is computed directly or composed from its hours; a property test checks that both agree for every field.
  - `docs/alarm-kpi-reference-cases.md` has 22 cases covering every case in exit criterion 1, recomputed by an independent brute-force Python script (it counts the window at every candidate instant, without the sweep the code uses) before the TypeScript ran; both agreed on the first run. The tests parse the six tables (occurrences, horizons, buckets, episodes, bad actors, revised hours); changing one digit fails a test.
  - The purity rule now lets a pure package import another pure package by its exact name; `alarm-core` imports `oee-core`. A subpath, `plant-config`, `contracts`, an app or a Node.js built-in is still refused (tested).
  - `config/plant/alarms.yaml` is loaded by `plant-config` outside `SIMULATION_FILES`. The simulator's hash test changes thresholds, definitions and positions and still gets the golden summary's `configSha256`; `pnpm test:golden` is byte-identical.
  - `alarmVocabulary` in `@shopfloor/contracts` lists what the plant can raise; the simulator now imports its calibration alarm constants from there.
  - Deviation (strengthening): the plan's revision rule for a late raise is [t, t + max(window)] plus its active span. `revisionSpans` also covers an earlier open occurrence of the same point that the late raise now supersedes, and every flood episode in flood during [t, t + window] to its end, because a late raise can join two episodes and change the id and peak of every later hour of the joined one (reference case 20). For a late clear the span runs from the raise to the later of the two ends, so the hours after the clear that showed the occurrence active are revised too (case 19), not only the raise→clear span.
  - Deviation: a flood episode's id is `{startAt}~{equipment}~{code}`, not the plan's (start, seq): `seq` is per device, and an id without it lets the ground truth and the MES agree by construction. Raises in one millisecond are ordered by equipment, then `seq` (within a device), then code.
  - Decisions the plan left open, recorded in ADR-0024: at one instant the raises that leave the window go before the raises that arrive, and the flood state is the count after both, so an episode goes on when its last counted raise leaves as another arrives; a superseded or epoch-ended occurrence is never fleeting; an open raise is undecided while raise + `fleetingMs` − 1 > `asOf`; in a closed epoch a running flood ends at the close.
  - Review (PR #150), adopted: a raise arriving at the instant a flood ends was counted as one of its raises (now [start, end), reference case 21); raise order and flood ids no longer compare sequence numbers across devices (case 22); `clearKind: 'lost'` is never fleeting; a bucket after `asOf` lists no open episode; the reviewer's revision-completeness fuzz is a property test; the OPC UA servers take their alarm codes from `alarmVocabulary`; ADR-0024 notes that floods are scope-wide, so a late fact revises position and plant hours.
  - `analysisConfigSha256` belongs to #140 (`sim-summary/3`), not here.
- #141: ingest, gateway and master data for alarms (ADR-0017 amended; UNS contract 1.2; register map 2).
  - Gateway: the alarm snapshot is published with every state snapshot. OPC UA reads `Epoch`, `Sequence` and the `Active` variables in one Read call; the codes come from `plantAlarms` (the machine's slice of the vocabulary). A Read with any value not Good publishes nothing; the next resync tries again. `sourceTime` is the `Sequence` value's source timestamp, the time of fact `asOfSeq`, so a lost clear closed at the snapshot's time is never later than the snapshot proves.
  - Contract change, additive: the WSH-01 map had only a heater-fault status bit (register 8), which is refreshed at read time and covers one code. Register 17 `activeAlarms` holds a bit per alarm table entry and is written in the same step as the ring entry and `eventCounter`, so one read of registers 0–17 is atomic. Map version 2; a version 1 reader of 0–16 is unaffected. The only simulator change is that register (`washer-server.ts`), apart from #140's files.
  - Topic: `_snapshot` is the one non-kebab topic name, allowed only under `alarms`, so it can never collide with an alarm code's topic.
  - Ingest: a retained alarm or a snapshot counts as `anchor` in the metrics. The newest epoch of an equipment is the greatest of its device stream's cursor, its state anchors' device epochs, its alarm anchors and its snapshots; staleness is stored, not computed on read, so every reader sees the same rule. A test starts ingest after a simulator restart with the old run's alarms and snapshot retained, in both arrival orders, and none is listed as active.
  - Deviation: the brief named the table `master_data.alarm_point`; the plan's `alarm_definition` is kept, with `alarm_position` and `alarm_position_work_center` for the positions. `alarmFacts` takes `fromSeq`/`toSeq` (inclusive), like `stateFacts`, for the plan's `from`/`to`.
  - Added beyond the plan: `alarmFactCounts()` for the alarms module's catch-up, and `alarmBaseline()` with the pure `resolveAlarmBaseline`, so the snapshot-versus-anchor rule is tested here, before #142 uses it.
- #140: simulator failure injection, alarm ground truth and the golden scenario `alarms-01`.
  - Injection as the plan defines it: a simulation event, applied only in `Running` (halt, cancel the pending clock event, `#breakDown`), held otherwise and applied in `#run`, where every path into `Running` passes, straight to `Down`. The per-state tests run each injection in the middle of a real interval of that state and compare with the same run without it: identical event logs up to the instant the plain run first enters `Running`, one breakdown exactly there, no zero-length `Running` interval anywhere, and the invariant checker after every step. All nine states pass (Running, Stopped, Starved, Blocked, Setup, Down, PlannedStop, Off, Maintenance).
  - Deviation: the transition table also gains `Down → Down`, beside the plan's `Off`, `PlannedStop` and `Maintenance` → `Down`. An injection held while the machine is already down (the plan's per-state test for `Down`) applies at the end of the repair, where the machine would enter `Running`; it stays `Down`, and the state event carries the injected code, so the MES opens a new interval with the new cause. Without it the second failure's time would carry the first failure's cause. The "never the same state" test now allows exactly this edge.
  - Deviation: the plan's held injection emits `injection-held`; the line also emits `injection-applied` at the breakdown, so the event log and `GET /api/v1/faults/injections` show both instants. The OPC UA and Modbus servers ignore both.
  - The calendar-boundary check lives in `plant-config` (`checkInjections`), as the plan says, so `plant-config` now depends on `@shopfloor/shift-calendar` (pure); the gateway's and the simulator's Dockerfiles copy the new workspace packages (`pnpm check:dockerfiles`).
  - Alarm ground truth: `AlarmTruth` uses `prepare`, `floodEpisodes`, `kpisOfBucket`, `composeBuckets`, `badActors` and `scopeOccurrences` from `alarm-core`. Its `seq` is the occurrence's index among its machine's alarm events (ADR-0024: any number that rises with the machine's emission order), so standing alarms are written without `seq`. Hours are listed only when something happened in them; shift buckets are every shift instance and every `no-shift` stretch, keyed `no-shift/{start of the stretch by the calendar}`, because standing and active-at-end need a contiguous bucket (the OEE section keeps one `no-shift` key). #142 must use the same keys.
  - `alarms-01` exact times (the issue left them to this PR): 13 injections on Cell M1 from 05:00:05Z to 05:04:40Z (repairs 3–5 s), LKT-01 `FIXTURE-SEAL` at 06:20:00Z (500 ms), PCK-01 `PRINTER-FAULT` at 13:30:00Z (33 h). Two washer injections and the printer fault were held while the machine was starved: the printer raises at 13:30:20.030Z and clears at Tuesday 22:30:20.030Z, standing at the end of Tuesday's shift B. Ground truth: 15 occurrences; Cell M1 shift A 13 raises, `peak10` 13, one flood window, one flood episode (05:03:15Z–05:10:25Z, peak 13), chattering point CNC-01 `COOLANT-PRESSURE`; one fleeting; one standing.
  - Deviation (review of PR #151): the alarm shift buckets key each `no-shift` stretch as `no-shift/{start}` (the calendar's start of the stretch; the run's start only if no shift ended in the 7 days before), while the OEE section and `/ground-truth`'s `buckets` keep one plain `no-shift`. Standing and active-at-end need a contiguous bucket. Recorded in ADR-0024 so #142 uses the same keys.
  - Review follow-ups (PR #151): the live test also compares the live event lines with the headless lines up to 11:00Z (identical); `runHeadless` refuses an injection outside [start, end) and the API answers 409 after the run has finished; the hour in progress is always listed (provisional), even when empty; `repairMs` is capped at 365 days.
  - Golden: `week-01/summary.json` regenerated for `sim-summary/3` (1,735 lines added, only the schema line changed), `events.sha256` unchanged; reason in `apps/simulator/golden/README.md`; owner approval deferred under the standing instruction. `alarms-01`'s event log (5,332 lines) equals `week-01`'s for the first 695 events, up to the first injection.
- #142: the `alarms` module (ADR-0024 "Occurrences in the MES"; migration `0012`).
  - Occurrences are derived by one pure function per (equipment, epoch) from the facts, the anchors without a stored fact, the snapshot once the stream is settled up to its `asOfSeq` and the epoch's close; the module keeps the open epochs' facts in memory, re-derives on every fact (in any order) and writes only the difference. Anchors and snapshots used while their epoch was the newest stay used after they become stale.
  - Golden reconciliation (`pnpm test:e2e`): `week-01` (112 comparisons: 2 occurrences; plant and both positions with their hours, 16 shift buckets, floods, run and shift bad actors) and `alarms-01` (129: 15 occurrences, a flood, chattering, fleeting and standing alarms) equal the ground truth exactly, in order and shuffled with 10 % duplicates, with revision 0 and no incident; shuffled delivery stores identical occurrences. `alarms-01` also reconciles intervals, OEE (48 buckets) and downtime records. The headless CLI gained `--scenario <name>` for it. Runtime: `week-01` about 82 s in order and 198 s shuffled (about 180 s without the alarms module), `alarms-01` about 12 s and 16 s.
  - The live reconcile (`pnpm test:reconcile`) compares shift A's alarm KPIs of the plant and both positions with the live ground truth and the golden week (all zero in `week-01`'s shift A; #147 adds the injections).
  - Deviation: the plan's `alarms.kpi_finality` (per equipment, epoch and hour, with raise and clear counts) became `alarms.kpi_revision` per (scope, epoch, hour) holding only revisions: finality is computed from final through on read, and `occurrence_source.final_through` keeps what was final for a late fact found at start-up. Revisions are per scope, as ADR-0024 requires for scope-wide floods.
  - Deviation: only a change that reaches an hour already final goes through `revisionSpans`: in a first version an in-order raise joining a flood begun in a final hour revised that hour, because the flood span starts at the episode's start (tested).
  - Deviation (exit criterion 2, "every bucket final"): a bucket is final once every machine of its scope is final through its end plus `fleetingMs`. `week-01`'s Friday shift C (every machine shuts down exactly at its end) and the weekend, and `alarms-01`'s last shift (the run ends with machines running), stay provisional, as the weekend does for OEE; their values still equal the ground truth, and every other bucket is asserted final.
  - Added beyond the plan: equipment scopes for KPIs and `alarm-kpi:equipment:<id>`; `GET /alarms/scopes`; `Equipment.epochEnd`. Not done here: `GET /alarm-points` (with the shelves of #143).
  - Review of PR #153, adopted: revisions were judged against "final through" after it moved (a snapshot applied by a gap write-off revised hours that had never been final) and, at start-up, against bounds the catch-up had already overwritten; both now use the bounds from before the change (regression tests P1 and P3, and a restart test where a late fact revises an hour final before the restart). `final_through` is stored once it passes an hour's end plus `fleetingMs`. Revisions commit in the same transaction as their occurrence rows. Index on `(epoch, ended_at)` for the KPI reads' lookback (migration 0012, not yet merged, edited in place). The scope sets of the MES and the ground truth are compared; catch-up's close falls back to the latest time the epoch knows; `--scenario` refuses `--seed`, `--start`, `--days` and `--config`.
  - Re-review of PR #153: a learned "final through" and an epoch's close are published only after the derivation they make applicable (a snapshot, the close) has committed, so a reader never sees an hour final with the rows from before, and a failed write leaves the old bounds for the retry (tests with an injected write failure).
- #143: alarm writes (ADR-0024 "Operator lifecycle", ADR-0023 note; migration `0013`).
  - Acknowledge single and bulk (up to 100, one transaction, one write for the rate limit) as review fix 13 asks: one `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING` into `alarms.occurrence_state`, audit rows only for what it inserted, no version. Four concurrent overlapping bulk requests acknowledge and audit each occurrence once (tested). Comments, shelve and unshelve per point with a version, all in the append-only `alarms.alarm_transition` with actor, `authenticated: false`, wall time, plant time and request id.
  - Shelf expiry in plant time: `until` = the plant clock (the newest epoch's watermark) at the write + `durationMs` (1 minute to `maxShelveMs`). After every ingest notice (in the module's queue) and at start-up, a shelf whose epoch's clock reached `until` ends `expire` (audited at `until`), and one whose epoch is older than the plant clock's ends `epoch-ended` (at the old epoch's last plant time), both by `system`. Tested to the millisecond (one ms before `until` still shelved), across a restart that missed the new epoch, and for a shelf of the second epoch ended by a third.
  - KPIs: `/alarms/kpi`, floods and bad actors are byte-identical before and after acknowledging, commenting, shelving and unshelving, `alarms.kpi_revision` unchanged, and equal to a twin database without writes after a shelf expired with a raise of the shelved point.
  - Deviation (decision): an occurrence ended by a newer epoch is `closed` without acknowledgement, so a restarted simulator's alarms never stay in the active list; any other ended occurrence stays `unacked-cleared` in the active list until acknowledged. `GET /alarms` views now partition the occurrences (`active`, `acknowledged`, `shelved`, `history` = closed); #142's `history` (every ended occurrence) changed with it, and two #142 tests were updated (a cleared occurrence is active until acknowledged).
  - Deviation (strengthening): `alarms.occurrence_state` is insert-only by a trigger, like the audit; comments are kept only in the audit (no separate table); a shelf's version also rises with the MES's own expiry and epoch end.
  - Deviation from the brief, following the plan: shelve and unshelve are `POST /alarm-points/{equipment}/{code}/shelve` and `…/unshelve` (the brief offered PUT and DELETE "or the equivalent"). Added: `GET /alarm-points/{equipment}/{code}/audit` and the `alarm-point` push message on the `alarms` topic (no new topic).
  - Not done here: the plan's MES-only measures beside the KPIs (shelved and annunciated raises, time to acknowledge, shelved time); they must not change `/alarms/kpi`, so they belong to a separate read with #145 or the shift report.
