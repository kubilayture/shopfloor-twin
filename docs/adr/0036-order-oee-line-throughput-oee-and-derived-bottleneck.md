# ADR-0036: Order OEE over job windows, line throughput OEE and a derived bottleneck

- Status: accepted; amended in #230 (P9-03): `notBefore` is part of the head time
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Deciders: Claude (#229), within the Phase 9 plan ("Order and line OEE", open decisions, review rounds 1–3 and the final check); recorded for the owner under the standing instruction of 2026-10-15

## Context and problem statement

Master plan section 7 asks for OEE "per order" from Phase 9, and ADR-0021 left the line-throughput OEE, its option (c), "until orders". Until now OEE exists per machine and per work center (time-weighted sums, ADR-0021 (b)); the bottleneck exists only as a web constant (`DESIGNED_BOTTLENECK = 'asm-01'`). The simulator's ground truth and the MES must compute order and line OEE identically from the same facts, so the definitions belong in `oee-core`, the pure, exact package both use. The questions:

- where the bottleneck comes from, without changing a golden: `oee.yaml` is hashed into every summary's `analysisConfigSha256`, the plant configuration into `configSha256` and every event log;
- what time an order owns, given that the CNC runs one job at a time but the downstream machines interleave both CNCs' orders;
- where a job's time starts, given that its changeover lies before its `started` fact (ADR-0030), and what happens when the fact that would say so is lost;
- how a line OEE is defined, and what happens when it exceeds 1;
- what quality an order reports, given that OP10's Q hides OP40's leak scrap.

## Considered options

1. **Bottleneck source:** (a) a key in `oee.yaml`; (b) a new configuration file; (c) derived in `oee-core` from the effective cycle (ideal cycle ÷ machines on the operation).
2. **Order OEE scope:** (a) every machine's time split between orders by their units; (b) the order's job windows on its CNCs from `started`; (c) the job windows from head time.
3. **Line OEE:** (a) ADR-0021's time-weighted sums; (b) good output against the bottleneck's ideal rate.
4. **Line OEE above 1:** (a) cap at 1; (b) report it with a flag.

## Decision outcome

Chosen: 1c, 2c, 3b and 4b.

### Bottleneck, derived (1c)

`deriveBottlenecks({ operations, equipment, idealCycleMs })` picks per product the operation with the largest **effective cycle**, its ideal cycle (`products.yaml`) ÷ the number of machines that run it (`plant.yaml`), ties to the earlier operation of the routing; the effective cycle is an exact fraction. OP10 runs on two CNCs: VB-100 OP10 240 s ÷ 2 = 120 s against OP30 150 s; VB-200 330 s ÷ 2 = 165 s against 210 s. So **ASM-01 for both products**, although OP10 has the largest raw cycle. It is the **designed** bottleneck and holds whatever the plant does: when only one CNC has work, the CNC is the actual constraint, and that shows as a lower line OEE, never as another bottleneck, so a bucket's ratio never changes what its numerator means (reference case L9). (a) changes `analysisConfigSha256` of every golden, (b) adds a file both sides must read for one fact the routing already implies. `apps/simulator/src/headless/config-hashes.test.ts` asserts both hashes unchanged for the four goldens (against the committed summaries and the pre-Phase-9 values) and that the derivation gives ASM-01 from each golden's configuration.

### Head time and job windows (2c)

- A job is the **head** of its CNC's queue from `max(its accepted fact, the previous job's completed fact on that CNC)` (`headTimes`, queue in acceptance order; and, by the amendment below, not before the dispatch's `notBefore`); both are job facts (ADR-0030) the ground truth and the MES see alike. The head time lies before the job's changeover and its `started` fact, so the changeover counts against the order that caused it. Idle `NO-ORDER` while a head job waits for its `notBefore` stays excluded from planned busy time (ADR-0021, `orders` rule).
- A lost `accepted` fact is replaced by the command result's `acceptedAt` (`uns.cmd-res/1`, the plant time of that same fact): the head time stays exact and the order can be final. A lost `completed` fact of the previous job is replaced by the end of its last OP10 cycle (`completionOf`; ADR-0030 defines `completed` as that instant; source `previous-last-cycle`), but only when the **attribution boundary is closed** and exactly `quantity` cycles are attributed to it. Under ADR-0031 a cycle belongs to the job whose `started` fact is the latest before it, so while the next job's `started` fact is missing, the next job's cycles are attributed to this one; the boundary is closed when the next job on the CNC has a known `started` fact, or when there is no later job. With the boundary open, or a count other than `quantity`, there is no recovered completion and nothing throws (reordered or lost facts never stop a rebuild; reference cases H14 and H15). Otherwise the head time is **unknown** (`headTimeUnknown`, with its reason): the window starts at the job's `started` fact, which leaves the changeover out, and the order's OEE is provisional. Only the **lost** reasons make it never final: `accepted-lost` (the fact lost and no command result) and `previous-completed-lost`. `accepted-unknown` (not received yet) and `previous-completed-missing` (the previous job's fact not received while this one has started) are transient: the order becomes final once the fact arrives. A job whose predecessor has not completed and which has not started is `waiting` and has no window.
- The **job window** (`jobWindow`) runs from the head time to the job's completion, or to the caller's cut-off while open (`open`, provisional): final-through on the MES side, the snapshot time in the ground truth. When the job's own `completed` fact is lost, its completion is the end of its `quantity`-th attributed OP10 cycle once all its cycles are known; otherwise (`endLost`) the window is capped at the next job's `started` fact on the CNC, so it never overlaps its successor, or runs to the cut-off (`open`) while there is none; either way it is provisional and `lost`. The cap charges the next job's changeover (between this job's last cycle and the next `started`) to this job; that is accepted because the window is flagged `lost` and never final (reference case O10). `jobWindows(queue, until)` computes head times and windows for one CNC's whole queue and pairs each job with its own successor, so the cap and the boundary can't come from another machine; `jobWindow` called directly refuses a `next` job whose `machine` differs. `jobWindows` takes the machine and refuses a job that names another one.
- **The queue contract:** the queue passed to `jobWindows` must hold every job dispatched to the CNC whose acceptance isn't refuted: the orders module's commands, including a job whose `accepted` fact and `acceptedAt` are both unknown. Such a job has no known `started`, so it keeps its predecessor's attribution boundary open. A queue built from job facts alone is wrong: one written-off gap can cover J1's last cycle, J1's `completed` and J2's `accepted` and `started`, and J2's cycles would then be attributed to J1, giving J1 a wrong window that could be finalised (reference case H16). A next job that is accepted but never starts keeps J1 `endLost` and open to the cut-off; accepted, because the window is flagged `lost`. A completion before the window's start is refused with an error naming the job (inconsistent facts). `JobWindow` and `OrderOeeResult` carry `headTimeUnknown`, the reason, `endLost` and `lost` (never final).
- **Why job windows:** the only time the plant attributes to one order without a rule of thumb. The CNC runs one job at a time; ASM-01's time split between two orders by units would need an allocation key the ground truth would have to invent. (b) would drop the changeover, which the order caused.

### Order OEE

`orderOee` applies the equipment rules (ADR-0021: loss table, whole-interval micro-stop threshold, `scheduledTime`) to the CNC's state intervals inside each window (`windowInputs` cuts them into a scheduled part, the shift buckets, and an unscheduled part, each an ordinary `BucketInput`; a Stopped interval keeps its whole length where the window cuts it, and an open one stays a provisional micro-stop) and sums the jobs' totals, time-weighted as a work center. Produced and good are the job's OP10 cycles, attributed by unit by the caller (ADR-0031); ideal time is the product's OP10 ideal cycle. A, P, Q and OEE are exact fractions with ADR-0021's edge cases; the result carries `open`, `pending` (a job without a window yet: waiting, or an unknown head time and no `started` fact), `headTimeUnknown`, `endLost` and `lost`, and `provisional` is set by any of them or by a provisional micro-stop, so an order with a job still pending is never reported complete. Finality beyond that (every job fact, unit and machine final) is the caller's (ADR-0018, ADR-0031), and an order with `lost` is never final.

**First-pass yield across the routing** (`firstPassYield`) is reported beside it, never multiplied in: units packed good at the last operation with every record good (no rework, no retest) ÷ units started. The order OEE's Q sees only OP10; FPY shows OP40's leak scrap and rework.

### Line throughput OEE (3b, 4b)

- **Line OEE** = Σ good packed units × the bottleneck's effective cycle per product ÷ line planned busy time (`lineOee`), per hour, shift bucket or order. One ratio, no A × P × Q split; the dashboard keeps the per-machine waterfall beside it. Null when the planned busy time is 0.
- **Line planned busy time** (`linePlannedBusyMs`): the bucket's scheduled time (the shift buckets) minus planned stops (the calendar's breaks and planned maintenance on the bottleneck, given by the caller as ranges); in orders mode only where at least one order is **in progress**, from its first job's head time (so its changeover counts) to its completion (ADR-0031): the line's counterpart of Idle `NO-ORDER`. Overlapping orders count once.
- **Per order:** the same ratio over the order's in-progress time with its own good units. Orders overlap, so per-order line OEE is **not additive** (reference cases L3 and L4); screens and the API say so.
- **Unknown in-progress start:** an order's in-progress time starts at its first job's head time. While that head time is unknown (`headTimeUnknown`, the window starting at `started`), every line bucket the order's in-progress time touches, and the order's own line OEE, are provisional; with a `lost` reason they are never final. The caller carries this flag beside `lineOee`'s result, as it carries finality (ADR-0018).
- **Above 1, not capped (4b):** a fast operator (`op-b1`, speed factor 0.95) or WIP drained from the buffers into a short bucket packs more than the bottleneck's ideal rate allows for the bucket's time. The value is reported as computed, flagged `aboveOne` (exactly 1 is not flagged). A cap would make the hours' sums disagree with their shift; a property test asserts that hours' planned busy time and ideal time sum to the shift's.
- ADR-0021's work-center OEE (a) stays as it is, beside the line OEE.

### Exactness and reference cases

Integers in, reduced BigInt fractions out, no floating point (the `exactnessRules` lint covers the new files). `docs/oee-reference-cases.md` gains cases B1–B4 (bottleneck: parallel machines, a tie, a fractional effective cycle), H1–H16 (head times and windows, including `acceptedAt`, a lost `accepted`, a lost `completed` capped at the next start or recovered from the last cycle, an open attribution boundary with and without loss, a next job of unknown acceptance, a transient missing `completed`, `waiting`, and the cut-off edges), O1–O9 (order OEE: one job, a changeover before `started`, two jobs on two CNCs, a shift boundary and a break, a micro-stop and a breakdown, a short order, `acceptedAt`, a lost head time, an open window, a lost end capped at the next start), F1 (first-pass yield) and L1–L13 (line OEE: no order in progress, overlapping orders, per order not additive, rework packed in a later bucket, above 1 from a fast operator and from buffer drain, exactly 1, one CNC working, planned maintenance, outside the schedule with an order in progress, a bucket and an order reaching past the schedule), hand-calculated to four decimals; `order-line-reference-cases.test.ts` parses them.

### Consequences

- Good: the ground truth (P9-03) and the MES (P9-04, P9-05) share one definition, so a difference can only come from data; no golden event log, summary or configuration hash changes.
- Good: the changeover is charged to the order that caused it, from facts both sides see; a lost acceptance is recovered from the command path, and what can't be recovered is flagged, never guessed.
- Good: line OEE above 1 is explained rather than hidden, and buckets add up to their shift.
- Bad: an order whose head time or end is lost is never final; with an unknown head time its OEE leaves out the changeover and looks better than it is (flagged).
- Bad: per-order line OEE is not additive across orders, and the order OEE covers only the CNC's time: downstream losses show in the line OEE and in first-pass yield, not in the order's A, P and Q.
- Bad: the designed bottleneck can understate a CNC-constrained bucket's performance against the CNC's own rate (by design: it is a lower line OEE, case L9).
- Bad: callers must pass planned stops (breaks and the bottleneck's planned maintenance) and in-progress ranges as ranges; both sides must derive them the same way, which the golden reconciliations (P9-04, P9-05) check.

## Amendment (P9-03, #230): `notBefore` is part of the head time

- **Change:** a job's head time is `max(accepted ?? acceptedAt, previous job's completion, notBefore)`, where `notBefore` is the dispatch's earliest start (`QueuedJob.notBefore`, optional). When `notBefore` is strictly later than both other terms, the head source is `notBefore`; a tie keeps the other term's source (`accepted`, `acceptedAt`, `previous-completed` or `previous-last-cycle`), as a tie between acceptance and the previous completion keeps the acceptance (reference cases H20 and H21). The rules for an unknown acceptance or a lost or missing previous completion are unchanged: `notBefore` alone never makes a head time known (H18), and a job whose predecessor is still open and which hasn't started is `waiting` whatever its `notBefore` (H19).
- **Why:** a job held by `notBefore` is waiting for demand, not losing time on the machine, and standard OEE keeps "no demand" out of planned time. Without the term, a job accepted early and queued behind a finished job was "in progress" from that job's completion: in `orders-01` PO-2610-007 and PO-2610-009 would have counted from Monday 16:19Z and 05:55Z to Tuesday 08:17Z, and Monday's shift C would have been planned (27 000 000 ms) with nothing packed, a line OEE of 0 that measures the order book, not the line. The order OEE was barely affected (the CNC's Idle `NO-ORDER` while it waits is excluded under `scheduledTime: orders`), but the order's window and its line in-progress time were wrong.
- **Both sides know it:** the MES sets `notBefore` in the dispatch command (ADR-0030), so its queue carries it from the command state (P9-04); the simulator's truth reads it from the line's job registry when the `accepted` event is recorded, so the event log and every `events.sha256` stay as they are.
- **Consequences:** an order's window and in-progress time start at its `notBefore` when it binds, so its changeover still counts (the CNC changes over once the job may start); a head time still after the cut-off doesn't start the order's in-progress time; reference cases H17 (`notBefore` after the previous completion, and one that doesn't bind) and O11 (an order window starting at `notBefore`). In `orders-01` Monday's shift C now has no planned line time (line OEE null), and `quality-01`'s PO-2610-106 is head from its `notBefore`, Tuesday 03:00Z.
- **Conventions stated for P9-04 and P9-05** (recorded in `docs/simulation-model.md`, "Ground-truth order and line OEE"):
  - order completion is ADR-0031 completion: the latest terminal record (packed good or scrapped), once every job is completed, every blank taken and every unit terminal;
  - planned maintenance is the Maintenance intervals of the bottleneck's machines;
  - the first-pass yield's denominator is blanks taken;
  - a head time still after the cut-off doesn't start an order's in-progress time;
  - every cut-off is exclusive (events at it belong to the next read); a cut at or before the start is an empty section; the live ground truth names the cut `lineOeeAsOf`;
  - golden replays carry no MES dispatch, so the MES harness (P9-04, P9-05) seeds the orders module's command state, `notBefore` included, from the run header's `dispatches`.
