# ADR-0018: Equipment intervals: provisional storage, watermark sources and catch-up

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Deciders: Claude (#96), within the Phase 4 plan; recorded for the owner under the standing instruction of 2026-10-15

## Context and problem statement

The Phase 4 plan fixes what an equipment state interval is (from one state fact, or the anchor, to the next fact of the same equipment and device epoch, by `seq`, on `sourceTime`), that every stored piece lies inside one UTC hour and one shift bucket, that the open interval is closed provisionally at the plant watermark, and that a late fact recomputes only the window between its neighbours. Implementing it left these questions open:

- **Provisional storage:** how is the open interval stored, and how often is it rewritten while the watermark moves (at 60× the plant sends about 25 messages a second)?
- **Watermark sources:** device facts carry the device epoch, but telemetry carries the gateway's epoch (`scope: topic`). Which epoch does a telemetry sample advance, and how does the watermark survive a restart?
- **Catch-up:** ingest's notices are a fast path that can be missed (ADR-0017). How does the module notice that, without scanning ingest's tables after every message?
- **Anchor versus facts:** which wins when both are known, and what if a device's time goes back within an epoch?

## Considered options

Provisional storage: (a) store only closed intervals and compute the open one on read; (b) store the open interval as pieces flagged `provisional`, rewritten as the watermark moves; (c) as (b), but in a separate table.

Watermark of telemetry: (a) ignore telemetry; (b) attribute it to the plant's newest device epoch; (c) attribute it to the newest device epoch of its own equipment; (d) the gateway names the device epoch in the sample, as in a snapshot.

Catch-up: (a) recompute everything at every start; (b) a periodic scan of ingest's tables; (c) record per (equipment, epoch) what the stored intervals were computed from, and compare with a per-epoch summary of ingest's tables at start-up.

## Decision outcome

- **Provisional storage: (b).** The open interval is stored up to the watermark of its epoch, cut at every hour and shift boundary passed, every piece flagged `provisional`; the next fact replaces it in the same statement that stores the closed interval. It is rewritten when the watermark passes an hour or shift boundary, or has moved `provisionalStepMs` (default 60 s of simulation time, one wall second at 60×) since the last write; a new fact always stores the exact watermark, and `flush()` does so for all. Only the pieces from the last stored one on are rewritten. (a) would make every reader (API, OEE, reports) repeat the cutting; (c) would make every query a union.
- **Closing:** a newer epoch of the equipment (any device fact, an anchor or a telemetry sample naming that device epoch) closes the old epoch's open interval at the old epoch's watermark at that moment, as final pieces. That end is recorded (`interval_source.closed_at`) and never moves again: nothing that arrives later for the old run, not even a late fact of another machine, extends a closed epoch.
- **Watermark: (d).** The watermark of a device epoch is the latest `sourceTime` of any device fact (state, cycle or alarm, any equipment) in that epoch, and of any telemetry sample whose `deviceEpoch` (UNS contract 1.1, #96) names it, unless its equipment has already left that epoch. A sample without `deviceEpoch` never moves a watermark. The gateway fills it from the same read as the value: the Modbus poller from the epoch register of the snapshot block it polls, the OPC UA collector from the `Epoch` its resync read on the current connection; after a connection loss, until that resync ran, samples go out without one. All simulated devices share the simulator's epoch, so a busy machine closes a silent one's interval; WSH-01's telemetry carries the simulator clock (`simTime`), so the plant's watermark moves even when every machine is off. (b) and (c) were wrong: the OPC UA collector's telemetry monitors deliver before its resync publishes the snapshot, so after a simulator restart the new run's samples were credited to the old epoch, and the #96 review saw an old interval stored as 46 hours of final Running (and, after an MES restart, old open intervals pushed a day forward). The first version of this ADR claimed the error was bounded by the gap until the new run's first fact; it isn't, since the new run's clock can be anywhere. There is no watermark table: at start-up it is the maximum of ingest's latest fact per epoch and the stored intervals' ends, so a telemetry-only advance survives through the stored open interval.
- **Catch-up: (c).** `equipment.interval_source` records, per (equipment, epoch), the number of state facts (counted from ingest for a reordered fact, so a fact handled twice isn't counted twice) and the last `seq` the stored intervals reflect, the anchor, whether the epoch is closed and whether times had to be clamped. At start-up, `Ingest.factSummary()` (one grouped pass over `ingest.equipment_event`) and `Ingest.anchors()` are compared with it; what differs is recomputed in full, the rest only has its open interval stored up to the watermark. A notice whose handling fails marks the (equipment, epoch) for a full recompute before the next notice; while it is marked, a fact never patches it from the stale state. Ingest re-announces a fact that the table had but its cursor didn't (a commit that reported failure but was applied, ADR-0017), with the epoch's anchor, which that commit may have attributed, so that path no longer loses a notice. (a) grows with history on every start; (b) costs a scan per period forever.
- **Windows:** a fact arriving in order (its `seq` above the last one known) extends the last interval without reading ingest's tables; otherwise `Ingest.stateFactWindow` returns it with its neighbours by `seq`, and only the pieces starting in [predecessor, successor) are replaced. Without a predecessor the window reaches back to the anchor. The pieces are upserted by key and the window's other pieces deleted in one statement, so a window is stored atomically in one round trip.
- **Anchor versus facts:** the anchor opens the first interval only if it is earlier than the first fact by time; otherwise facts drive the intervals and the anchor is ignored. The first anchor per (equipment, device epoch) wins (ADR-0017).
- **Time going back within an epoch** (a device clock bug, never the simulator): a fact earlier than the one before it is moved up to that time, so the earlier fact's interval is dropped as zero-length, and the (equipment, epoch) is marked `clamped`. An in-order fact is clamped against its predecessor directly; a reordered one triggers one full recompute the first time, and afterwards its window is clamped against the latest earlier time (`Ingest.latestStateTime`). Only a late fact whose time would move its successors forces another full recompute. The first version recomputed a clamped track in full on every fact, inside ingest's acknowledgement path: quadratic, and one bad clock stalled all ingest (#96 review).

### Consequences

- Good: readers see one table of pieces, already cut by hour and shift, with the open interval up to the watermark; provisional time is visible (`provisionalMs` in shift totals).
- Good: a missed notice or an MES restart costs a recompute of the affected (equipment, epoch), found by a single pass over the facts at start-up.
- Good: the in-order path costs one statement per state fact (about 1.4 ms on a local database, measured); a reordered fact one query more.
- Bad: the start-up summary scans `ingest.equipment_event` once; it grows with history (about 30,000 facts per simulated week). Retention or a per-epoch summary table can bound it later.
- Bad: between steps the stored open interval lags the watermark by up to `provisionalStepMs` of simulation time; readers that need the exact end use `currentState().until` or `flush()`.
- Bad: telemetry from a gateway older than contract 1.1, or sent between a reconnect and the resync, doesn't move any watermark; open intervals then wait for the next fact or a sample that names its run.
- Bad: a closed epoch's end depends on when the newer epoch was first seen, so it is arrival-dependent by design; a replay that delivers the new run earlier closes the old one earlier.
- **Retention:** catch-up recomputes an (equipment, epoch) whenever ingest's facts differ from what the intervals were computed from. A future retention policy that deletes old rows of `ingest.equipment_event` would therefore make the next start-up recompute, and so delete, the intervals of those facts. Retention must be designed with this in mind (for example retaining whole epochs, or marking epochs as archived in `interval_source` so catch-up leaves them alone).
