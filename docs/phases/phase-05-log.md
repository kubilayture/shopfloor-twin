  - New cross-checks: shifts on whole hours, time zones with whole-hour offsets (checked over every transition from 1970 to 2100), ideal cycle times in whole milliseconds.
  - Beyond the issue: in every bucket with PBT > 0 the losses satisfy PBT = APT + own + external and APT = micro-stops + speed + ideal (property-tested), and the quality loss closes the waterfall (ideal − quality loss = OEE × PBT).
  - Review (PR #128), adopted: cycles in an unscheduled bucket counted towards ideal, good and produced, so its speed and quality losses were non-zero while A, P, Q and OEE were null, and a day sum over shift and no-shift buckets would inflate P and OEE. - #113: interval finality per piece (ADR-0018, amended).
  - "Final through" per (equipment, device epoch) is the latest time of the machine's own device facts with no open sequence gap below them; a gap reported as an incident counts as closed, and a closed epoch is final everywhere. A piece is provisional exactly when it ends after that time, open or closed interval alike. `equipment.bucket_finality` (migration `0005`) records per (equipment, epoch, UTC hour) when the hour became final, its revision and the gap incidents over it; shift totals report `revision` and `incidents` per row, and `provisionalMs = 0` for a completed bucket whose machine reported after it.
  - Module boundary: ingest's fact notices carry `settledSeq` from the in-memory cursor, a sweep sends a `gaps` notice, and `Ingest.settled` answers the rest. In order, final through moves without a query; only jumps (a hole filled or reported) read.
  - Deviation: a stream the MES first sees above seq 1 now holds finality back below its first number for the gap grace period, then writes that history off without an incident. Without it, the shuffled golden week granted finality from whichever fact came first and then revised hundreds of hours.
  - Learned: the promotion bound was off by one (a piece ending exactly on the bound kept its flag); the shuffled replay found it, the in-order one didn't.
  - Golden week: every shift bucket is final except where a machine is silent at the end of the run (the weekend's `no-shift` bucket); no revision, no incident, in order and shuffled. The live reconciliation drops "totals stable", runs on until every machine has sent a fact after the shift end (bounded by `RECONCILE_SILENT_WAIT_MS`, reported as `silentWait`), and requires `provisionalMs = 0`, `revision = 0` and `incidents = 0` for shift A.

- #120: the `oee` module and its read API (ADR-0021, ADR-0022).
  - **Module.** `apps/mes-server/src/modules/oee` owns no tables and reads the other modules through their `index.ts` only. Per (machine, epoch, UTC hour) it takes time per state from `state_hourly`, the Stopped pieces with their whole interval's length, and cycles from `cycle_hourly`; a shift bucket is the sum of its hours (`bucketTotals` per hour, `sumTotals`), a work center the sum of its machines. Epoch: the one asked for, or per machine the newest with data in the range.
  - **Consistent reads.** `Equipment.oeeInputs` reads the hourly rows and the stop pieces of all machines in one REPEATABLE READ, read-only transaction, and the module derives each hour's Stopped time from the pieces it read, so `bucketTotals`' check can't throw on a race or on a stale materialised row (a dirty hour is not final anyway).
  - **Stop pieces.** `Equipment.stopPieces` finds each piece's whole interval by the next interval's first piece (pieces of a track are contiguous), or the track's last piece for the last interval. A stop is open while it is its epoch's last interval or its end lies after "final through"; its length then runs to "final through", never short of the piece (ADR-0021 note).
  - **Finality.** A bucket is `final` when every member has its last hour, every hour is final in equipment (`stateHourly.final`) and refreshed in `cycle_hourly` (`cyclesRefreshed`), and no open stop is still counted as a micro-stop (`result.provisional`). `provisionalMs` is the provisional time of the pieces, `revision` the highest hour revision, `incidents` the distinct gap incidents (`StateHour` gained `incidentIds`).
  - **API.** `GET /oee`, `/shifts/{date}/{shift}/oee`, `/shifts` and `/shifts/current` (instances from the calendar on the server), `/reason-codes`, `/downtime` (`uncoded=true`: only UNCODED records; ids `{equipment}~{epoch}~{startMs}`), `/downtime/pareto` (loss by reason, own and external; micro-stops by cause). Results use the ground truth's JSON shape (`resultDto` equals the simulator's `resultJson`), so a reconciliation is one equality.
  - **Push.** `oee:<id>`: a member's `piecesChanged` or `cycle` notice only marks the topic dirty; a timer computes dirty topics at most once a second and sends a delta only when the JSON changed; a new subscriber's snapshot comes from the next computation. `downtime`: the feed notes the changed hours per machine and, 250 ms later, reads their records and compares them with those it knows (created, closed, recoded, updated, removed). Equipment writes records in the same statement as their pieces, so no record change goes unnoticed without touching equipment's write paths.
  - **Golden week** (end-to-end job), in order and shuffled with 10 % duplicates, gap grace 1 h (above the replay's length): all 128 buckets (6 machines and 2 work centers × 16 shift buckets: 15 shifts and the weekend) equal the ground truth exactly; every scheduled bucket final with `provisionalMs`, `revision` and `incidents` 0; the no-shift OEE null on both sides. Also: Σ hour buckets = shift bucket for every machine and shift; the stop pieces per hour (ms, whole length) equal the ground truth's hours; each machine's shift Pareto equals its availability and micro-stop losses. The Friday-night test (`pnpm test`) runs the same checks on 24 buckets.
  - **Live** (`pnpm test:reconcile`): after the drain the run polls `GET /api/v1/shifts/2026-10-12/A/oee` (at most 30 s) until all six machines are final, then compares inputs and results with the live ground truth's `oee`, per machine and work center. Local run: RECONCILE-OK, all six machines and both work centers equal, final after 58 ms with `provisionalMs`, `revision` and `incidents` 0 (shift A OEE: CNC-01 and CNC-02 0.9778, WSH-01 0.4556, ASM-01 0.9611, LKT-01 0.5767, PCK-01 0.1911; Cell M1 0.8037, Line A1 0.5763).
  - **Web.** `live-cache.ts` ignores the new `oee` and `downtime` messages until the OEE screens (#122).

- #121: downtime coding writes with guards and audit (ADR-0023; ADR-0021 amended; migration `0009`).
  - **Model.** `equipment.downtime_coding` holds ranges by time with reason, comment, `coded_by` and the version they were written at; `equipment.downtime_coding_set` holds the version per (equipment, epoch); `equipment.downtime_audit` is append-only by a trigger (the MES owns the table, so a REVOKE would not bind it). Non-overlap of the codings is enforced under the set's row lock, not by an exclusion constraint (that needs `btree_gist`).
  - **Writes.** `PUT /api/v1/downtime/{id}/coding` replaces the codings inside one record; `GET /api/v1/downtime/{id}/audit` lists the record's writes and the clips of codings written to it, newest first. The `DowntimeCoding` class lives in the equipment module, which owns the tables; master data's reasons reach it as a `codable` callback.
  - **Re-cuts.** The coding module follows `onPiecesChanged` (debounced 100 ms) and clips codings within a day of the changed hours to the records; kept parts stay one per record, the rest is stored `orphaned` and audited by `system`. Start-up clips every set once. Readers clip codings to the record they read, so the short window before a clip counts nothing twice.
  - **Reasons in effect.** A record's `reason` is the reason over most of its time (the earliest on a tie); `segments` gives them by time; an open record never counts as fully coded. `Equipment.downtimeLoss` splits each record's clipped time between codings and the auto reason in SQL, still without a row limit. OEE reads no coding: the test compares the `/oee` answers (shift and hours) byte for byte before and after a recode and a split.
  - **Guards** (ADR-0023): Host; `MES_WRITES=off` (405, before any other write guard); `Origin` present and same host and port, or allowlisted (403); `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin` when sent (403); `application/json` (415); 16 KiB (413); a global token bucket of 10/s, bursts of 20 (429 with `Retry-After`). A request id (UUID) per request is returned as `X-Request-Id` on writes and audited.
  - **Proxies.** nginx: `limit_except GET POST PUT DELETE` and `client_max_body_size 16k` under `/api/`. Vite: the Origin rule moved into `apiProxyListeners` (`dev-proxy.ts`), used for `proxyReq` and `proxyReqWs`, with tests of both.
  - **Tests.** `coding.test.ts` (16 tests): every refusal path, no CORS headers (also on a preflight), allowlisted origin and the global limit, `MES_WRITES=off`, coding with a partial coding, a stale-version 409, a split and a clear, 422s, the audit with request ids and its append-only trigger, Pareto change with identical OEE, the `recoded` push with no OEE delta, clipping with a late fact that moves the end, and a whole coding orphaned across a restart.
  - Decisions beyond the plan: a clip raises the set's version, so a screen holding clipped codings gets 409 instead of overwriting; 422 (not 400) for a well-formed write that doesn't fit the record; an allowlisted origin on another port is still refused by `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-site` from a browser (the allowlist serves proxies and scripts).
  - Stack smoke (local, Colima): the new checks pass through nginx: a coding written and cleared with the page's origin (and both audited), a foreign and a missing `Origin` 403, a form content type 415, PATCH refused by nginx (403).
  - The push feed reports `recoded` when a record's codings change, not when only the set's version moved (a write to another record of the same machine).
